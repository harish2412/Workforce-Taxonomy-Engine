01What we built

The problem. Sprint 2 asks each team to turn free-text job postings into atomic skill statements that later sprints can cluster into a taxonomy. Our component is the rule + NLP extractor for the skills group.

What it does. For every posting it finds phrases that name a skill, cleans them, splits lists into single skills, removes repeats inside the posting, and stores one row per skill linked to the posting's job_id.

postings.csv→ load & dedupe texts→ normalize_text→ spaCy parse→ sentence labels→ Pass 1: rules→ atomic split→ Pass 2: noun chunks→ cleaning→ dedupe→ Parquet
StageWhat happens
Loadload_postings joins description and skills_desc, drops the 7 empty texts, and hashes each (company, text) pair so identical texts are parsed once.
Preprocessingnormalize_text restores line and sentence breaks lost when HTML was stripped (glued words, bullets, numbered lists). Each line becomes its own sentence.
spaCyen_core_web_sm tokenizes, tags parts of speech, parses dependencies (for noun chunks and sentences) and runs NER (used only as a filter).
Sentence labelsEach sentence is marked boilerplate or not (EEO, pay, benefits, how to apply) and given a section: requirements, responsibilities, skip or other.
Pass 1, rulesA "Skill: N years" regex, 41 forward trigger phrases ("experience with", "ability to"…) and backward heads ("… skills", "… experience").
Atomic splitLists after a trigger are split at commas, parentheses, "such as", and at "and/or" when it is clearly a list.
Pass 2, NLPspaCy noun chunks that do not overlap anything Pass 1 used, after section, NER, generic-word, role-word and pronoun filters.
Cleaning_clean_tokens strips filler at both ends and rejects generic, too short, too long, pronoun and URL candidates.
DedupeOne row per lowercased statement per posting; the rule version wins over the noun-chunk version.
OutputRows are copied back to every job_id that shares the text, numbered, and written to Parquet with a stats JSON.

What comes out. outputs/skill_statements_full.parquet: 3,738,856 rows. Each row has the skill, the posting it came from, the method and trigger, the section, a confidence label and the evidence sentence.

Whitespace runs collapse to one space, empty lines are dropped, and a custom spaCy component (newline_sentences) forces a sentence break at every line.

Side effect found: the numbered-list repair also removes a digit after a colon. Schedule:8 hour shift became Schedule:⏎hour shift.

Kept on purpose: letter case (needed to recognise proper nouns and acronyms), punctuation (commas and parentheses drive list splitting; + and # keep C++ and C#), stop words (the parser needs them) and numbers in the text (only stripped from the start of a statement).

Not implemented: HTML tag stripping (the text has no tags), Unicode normalization (curly ’ and straight ' stay different), spelling correction.

How a trigger is processed

QuestionWhat the code does
DetectionspaCy PhraseMatcher(attr="LOWER") (case-insensitive). filter_spans keeps the longest match when matches overlap. Triggers inside boilerplate sentences are skipped.
How much is capturedTokens after the trigger, within the same sentence: at most 14 for noun triggers, 24 for verb triggers.
Where capture stopsPunctuation . ; : ! ? | • or a newline · the start of another trigger · stop words (that, which, who, where, while, when, because, is/are/was/were, will/would/should/must/may, required, preferred, plus, highly, etc, but, coupled, combined, along, together…) · "as well as", "a plus" · noun triggers also stop at within, across, through, for, from, at, on, into, under, via and at "to" + verb · verb triggers also stop at parentheses
CommasNoun lists always split at , ; ( ) [ ], including, such, e.g., i.e., like, especially, particularly, namely, and/or. Verb phrases split at commas only if every piece starts with a verb and has 2+ tokens.
"and" / "or"In noun lists they split only if the span has a comma, or the left part has 2+ tokens, or the left word is a proper noun. "health and safety" stays whole; "Excel and Word" splits.
BulletsEvery bullet is its own sentence after preprocessing, so a capture never runs into the next bullet.
Not swallowing sentencesToken caps, stop words and punctuation, word limits per statement (8 for noun items, 12 for verb phrases), and a 100-character limit.

What qualifies as a noun-chunk skill

A doc.noun_chunks entry is kept only if it passes all of these, in order:

  1. Its sentence is not boilerplate and not in a "skip" section (company intro, benefits, pay, physical demands, EEO).
  2. It contains no . ! ? ,.
  3. No token was already used by Pass 1.
  4. No token belongs to a GPE, LOC, PERSON, DATE, TIME, MONEY, PERCENT, CARDINAL, ORDINAL, QUANTITY, FAC or NORP entity.
  5. No pronoun (you, our, it, who…).
  6. The head noun is not a pronoun or number, not in GENERIC_HEADS (~220 words such as team, environment, opportunity) and not in ROLE_HEADS (~120 job-title words such as manager, nurse).
  7. The first word is not in ACTION_VERBS (catches Title Case task headings like "Submit Orders").
  8. It survives cleaning with chunk limits of 2–5 words. A single word survives only if it is a known tool (capitalized, in KNOWN_SINGLE_TERMS), an acronym of 2–6 capitals not in BLOCKED_ACRONYMS, CamelCase, or contains + or #.
  9. It is not the company name, a location part, or contained in the job title.
  10. A single-word chunk is dropped if it starts its sentence or sits in a heading line.

Why spaCy helps and why it is noisy. Noun chunks find every noun phrase, which is how bullet-list skills get caught. But most noun phrases in a posting are tasks, objects or company talk. 11Deduplication

  • Within one posting: _dedupe keeps the first row per statement_norm. Rows are created in the order years-regex → forward triggers → backward → noun chunks, so the rule version wins. Verified: 0 duplicate (job_id, statement_norm) pairs in the full file. On the sample, 857 rows were dropped (624 chunk-after-chunk, 152 trigger-after-trigger, 81 chunk-after-trigger).
  • Rule vs NLP: Pass 2 also skips any chunk that touches a token Pass 1 used, so most overlap never reaches dedupe.
  • Case and whitespace: _norm lowercases and collapses whitespace, so variants share one key while statement keeps the original spelling.
  • Across postings: not deduplicated, by design. "customer service" appears in 13,545 postings and has 13,545 rows. For every top skill, occurrences equal postings.
  • Identical posting texts: parsed once and copied to each posting (108,467 parses for 123,842 postings).

13One row, traced end to end

Row 921716_006: statement Photoshop, method trigger, trigger "proficient in", section responsibilities, confidence high.

  1. Original posting text: "…cool-under-pressure. Please, be proficient in Adobe Creative Cloud (Indesign, Illustrator, Photoshop) and Microsoft Office Suite. Above all…"
  2. Normalization: this sentence is unchanged; other parts of the posting were repaired ("inclusion.Who you are:You" → "inclusion. Who you are: You").
  3. Matcher: "proficient in" matches (noun trigger, proficiency group). The sentence is not boilerplate.
  4. _capture_forward: collects tokens until "." → raw_span "Adobe Creative Cloud (Indesign, Illustrator, Photoshop) and Microsoft Office Suite".
  5. _split_list: splits at (, ,, ), and at "and" because the span has commas → five items.
  6. _clean_tokens: "Photoshop" has nothing to strip, is 1 word (noun limit 1–8) and is not generic → kept.
  7. _row: statement_norm "photoshop", sentence stored.
  8. Labels: section "responsibilities", set by the earlier "Job description" heading; "Who you are:" sits mid-line, so it is not detected as a heading. This mislabel is a known weakness. Confidence high because the method is trigger.
  9. _dedupe: first "photoshop" in this posting → kept.
  10. run: merged back to job 921716 and numbered 6th, because the two years-regex rows (Marketing, Graphic design) are created first; kind = "skill".
Data quirk: this posting's skills_desc describes a different job ("College or Graduate Student… Real Estate…"). Row 008, "work in an extremely fast paced environment", comes from that appended text.

21Quality assessment

ComponentVerdictDetail
Rule extractorgood, with holesLists, parentheticals, "such as" and years lines work well, and high-confidence rows are mostly real skills. "ability to" phrases include physical requirements and odd clauses; backward "skills" sometimes yields generic rows ("right skills"). Holes: lists after ":" or "at least", the 6-token backward cap, "Proficiency" without "in".
NLP extractoruseful but noisyFinds skills with no cue (UAT, wave planning, CI/CD pipelines) and covers 9,294 no-trigger postings. Many low rows are topics, tasks or form fields. NER mislabels skills (Java as PERSON, Spanish as NORP).
Cleaningworks, with systematic bugsRemoves pronouns, generic heads, boilerplate and long phrases well. Bugs: filler words damage real names (deep learning, Basic Life Support, KPIs, general ledger); the single-word rule is too strict; glued digits survive; apostrophes are not unified.
Atomic splittinggood for comma listsMulti-word names stay whole. Weak for "X and Y skills" and slash lists like "Tableau/Power BI".
Combined outputusable with filtering99.5% posting coverage and a strong high tier (1.07M rows). The medium and low tiers need filtering before clustering. These are judgements from inspection, not measured precision.

22Manual validation

NOT COMPLETED YET. No posting has been hand-labelled, and there is no labelling code. There are no precision, recall or F1 numbers. The tool coverage figures in section 20 are a heuristic check only.

What the roughly 100-posting evaluation still needs:

  1. Pick 100 postings, stratified like the sample but kept separate from the 400 used for tuning.
  2. Write short labelling rules: what counts as a skill, whether education and licences count, and how partial matches are scored.
  3. Two people label the skills in every posting independently, with some postings labelled by both to measure agreement.
  4. Compute precision (extracted rows judged correct) and recall (gold skills found), overall and by method and confidence.
